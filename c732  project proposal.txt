C732 Team Project Proposal
Mingran Chen(mca410), Wei Cai(wca162) Yun Tian(yta114) Zehua Li(zla330)
Dataset
Our project will focus on monthly updated flight data on US domestic flights from the Bureau of Transportation Statistics. It contains 109 different fields that covers information including date, aircraft information, airline information, origin, destination, delay causes, ground movement, etc. This dataset is chosen because 
   * It contains a large number of detailed flight-level records, allowing us to evaluate how our pipeline scales as the amount of data increases. 
   * It has a consistent structure and is updated monthly, making it easier to combine historical records and incorporate new data. 
   * It is publicly available, allowing us to obtain the data without paid access and make our analysis reproducible. 
   * It provides opportunities for meaningful data cleaning, including handling missing values, cancelled flights, and time-related fields. 
Analysis
        We want to explore the relationships between flight delay information and other fields within this dataset (and other datasets if one arises) . Specifically, we aim to examine the following relations:
   * Delay propagation: Does a late arrival cause further delays on the same aircraft’s later flights? If so, can we predict the delay time on the later flights?
   * Reliability: Can we rank the reliability of airports, routes, or airlines based on delay information?
   * Delay factors: Which factors are associated with the highest probability of a flight delay, and which are associated with the longest average delay? We will include flight origin and destination airport weather for this question. https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/cgi-bin/request/asos.py 
For each question, we will first examine the dataset to find the related fields for each data, and clean the dataset accordingly. This first part would take 2-3 days to complete. Then, researching and implementing the solutions to the above questions would take 3-4 days. 


Product
        We will develop an interactive visualization dashboard to present our analysis of flight data through graphs and tables. The dashboard will highlight key patterns, trends, and comparisons identified in our analysis. We estimate that this will take 4 days to complete. Then, we will develop one or two predictive features if the data demonstrates sufficient predictive value. Any predictive models will be evaluated on held-out data and compared against simple baseline methods to assess their performance. Implementing and training our model will take the remaining 1.5 weeks.


Technology
        We will use PySpark to extract, transform, and load (ETL) the flight data, with Amazon S3 providing storage for raw data and processed results. Apache Spark workloads will run on Amazon EMR clusters to support scalable processing and analysis. Additional technologies for visualization tools, Matplotlib(on Jupyter Notebook) and predictive modelling may be introduced as the project requirements evolve.
